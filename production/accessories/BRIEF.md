## 2. Creative treatment

### Premise

A pike angler decides to travel light: just one lure in a tiny clear case. When the angler lifts a favourite soft bait, its treble-hook stinger brings half the tackle box with it. Instead of spending the morning untangling everything, the angler disconnects the entire stinger, neatly packs the now-hookless bait, and appears to have solved the problem. The final wide shot reveals the whole tangled collection attached to the fishing bag beside that single-lure case. The other lures have been reclassified as “accessories.”

The humour is not that hooks get tangled; that is the familiar setup. The joke is that the angler is so committed to the fiction of packing light that they invent a loophole. Play it as a tiny, dead-serious heist film in which every action is performed with the competence of someone making an objectively ridiculous decision.

**Audience:** Pike and predator anglers who use large soft baits, stingers and treble hooks; the physical action must also be intelligible to casual viewers.

**Opening image:** A single green soft bait held vertically above an open tackle tray, with three other lures hanging beneath it like an absurd mobile. The cluster is already visible at frame zero, not hidden behind a slow setup. A tiny transparent travel case sits beside the tray, visibly too small for the bundle. Opening text: `Just one lure today.`

**Central question:** How is the angler going to fit that mess into the little case? Give the audience the tangle immediately, but withhold the solution and the eventual second storage location.

**Emotional progression:** Recognition → disbelief at the connected cluster → satisfaction at the clever disconnection → apparent victory → recognition of the ridiculous loophole.

**Ending image:** A waist-level, wider view of the bag: a neat transparent case containing exactly one green soft bait in one pocket, a conspicuous multicoloured cluster of other lures secured separately to the bag. Final text: `Accessories don't count.` The character pats the tiny case with satisfaction, then walks out. No wink to camera.

**Visual storytelling:** The lure cluster, the detachable rig, the small case and the bag are the four narrative objects. The viewer should understand the story from the changes in their physical relationships, even with all overlays removed. Text establishes intention and gives the final action its absurd justification; it must not narrate intermediate events.

### Concept selection and adversarial review

Three distinct premises were considered:

| Premise | Strongest element | Failure test / decision |
|---|---|---|
| A missing car key is recovered from a nest of pike lures | Physical surprise; familiar angler disorder | Requires a contrived lost-key setup and either repeats the snag or relies on an arbitrary lost-object punchline. Rejected. |
| Pike lure retirement home: a damaged favourite is formally retired, then selected instead of a new lure | Recognizable attachment to proven baits; potential deadpan performance | Display-case imagery may read as a slideshow, and choosing the old lure is predictable. Rejected. |
| Packing one lure while its treble rig drags other lures along | Instant large visual gag; specific pike tackle; practical mechanism | Initially risked becoming a familiar tangle meme. Selected only after giving the character a credible partial solution and a more absurd final decision. |

**Initial draft:** Lift one lure, reveal a tangled cluster, attempt to untangle it, fail, resign oneself to taking the whole tackle box. The opening worked, but the middle could be rearranged freely and the ending merely repeated the opening problem. The viewer would likely understand the entire joke in the first two seconds. Camera movement could not compensate for that structural weakness.

**Actual revisions:**

- Replace repeated untangling attempts with one specific, visible mechanical solution: detach the stinger while keeping its passengers connected. This makes the middle causal rather than interchangeable and introduces a natural product-category detail without a sales pitch.
- Introduce the single-lure clear case during the opening, making the character's goal physically measurable. The overhead shot and latch click provide a satisfying false ending rather than an empty pause.
- Replace resignation with a deliberate loophole. The angler succeeds at packing exactly one bait, then independently carries the rig and its passengers. The final wide juxtaposition reinterprets the initial promise, instead of simply showing more tangled hooks.
- Remove explanatory voice-over and a logo/CTA outro. Retain two lines of copy only. The final phrase adds the character's rationalization while the physical arrangement provides the visual joke.

**Fresh-viewer simulation (self-review, not blind or independently validated):** Frame zero must be legible as several separate lures rather than a heap of metal. The opening line establishes why that is funny. S02 provides new information (the tiny case) rather than another view of the same tangle. S03–S04 introduce the possibility of a solution, which is genuinely paid off. S05 is a deliberate relief beat. The reach back in S06 needs to be quick and unambiguous, otherwise the viewer might interpret the latch as the ending. S07 is the weakest remaining beat: the clip action could read as mundane bag packing or reveal the final gag early. Keep it tight, short and focused only on the attachment; the wide shot must deliver information genuinely absent from the insert. S08 should be an immediate visual contradiction before the final overlay is read. If the final case is not visible or the cluster looks too small, the joke collapses into normal packing.
