# Why Pike Attack Fishing Lures — Northern Stingers 001

## Evidence review (26 November 2026)
The requested opening is treated as an angler's hypothesis, immediately questioned,
not a demonstrated result about a satiated pike. No source reviewed establishes
that hunger is unnecessary for an artificial-lure strike. A bite cannot measure
subjective appetite. Do not market this film as proof of non-feeding aggression,
a territorial reflex, or a guaranteed lure technique. The final short investigates
observable cues rather than assigning a motivation to every strike.

Sources, paraphrased rather than quoted:

1. [Larsen et al., Fisheries Research 283 (2025), 107299](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.fishres.2025.107299).
Northern pike field experiment: artificial and natural baits elicited different
approach/attack patterns; approaches need not become bites. Individual behaviour
and satiation are possible influences. The paddle-tail explanation is the authors'
interpretation, not a controlled isolation of each visual cue. Supports the
uncertainty at the hook, follow-without-biting beat, and final insight.
2. [Savino & Stein (1989), Behavioural interaction between fish predators and their prey: effects of plant density, USGS record](https://www.usgs.gov/publications/behavioural-interaction-between-fish-predators-and-their-prey-effects-plant-density).
Controlled pool observations: northern pike used ambushing across tested vegetation
densities. Supports hiding at the weed edge, not a claim that every pike always hides.
3. [New, Fewkes & Khan (2001), JEB 204, 1207–1221](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11222136/).
Vision and lateral-line experiments were on **muskellunge**, a related Esox, not
northern pike. Supports the general sensory explanation, with the species limit
explicit here. Does not prove a lateral line detects a lure at long distance.
4. [Esociformes synthesis, US Forest Service hosted chapter](https://www.fs.usda.gov/rm/pubs_journals/2020/rmrs_2020_mccormick_f001.pdf).
Summarizes esocid visual acquisition, close-range mechanosensation, stalking and
powerful lunges. Supports the bounded, near-field water-motion explanation.
5. [Digital dissection of pike and eel (2019), Journal of Anatomy](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6637441/).
Pike feeding involves mouth expansion and suction. Supports opening jaws during
the lunge; avoid a snapping crocodile depiction or a long underwater roar.
6. [FishBase northern pike diagnosis](https://www.fishbase.org/summary/esox-lucius.html).
Long snout and large mouth; posterior dorsal/anal configuration informs original
art. Pale flank spots, olive body and forked tail distinguish the pike silhouette.

## Editorial decisions
The hook is verbatim, immediately followed by “Or can we really tell?” This is an
investigation of that claim; it does not certify the premise. Visual changes are
possible prey cues, not instructions that compel a bite. Wakes are represented by
particles and subtle plant displacement, never visible sonar circles. The rapid
lunge is an illustrative stylized event, not a measured kinematic reconstruction.
No fish injury, landing sequence, performance promise, captions or sales card.
